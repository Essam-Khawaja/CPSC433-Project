\section{Comparison of the Proposals}
\label{sec:integration}

We will implement whichever proposal the instructor, Cooper, selects.

Both optimization examples start from the timetable found
in Section~\ref{sec:or-example}. Under the preferences defined
in those examples, its penalty is $5$. Set-based search
reduces this to $3$ and then $2$. AND-tree search also reaches
$2$ and establishes that no better timetable exists for this
instance. The set-based stopping condition establishes only
that no valid Move or Swap improves its result.

These examples demonstrate the search steps, not performance
on larger inputs.
