\section{Overall Search Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{How the models relate}
\label{sec:relate}

We propose two complete search systems that share the same first stage:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Proposal A:} OR-tree for an initial valid timetable (Section~4) $\rightarrow$ set-based improvement (Section~5).
  \item \textbf{Proposal B:} OR-tree for an initial valid timetable (Section~4) $\rightarrow$ AND-tree optimization (Section~6).
\end{itemize}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{array}{ccc}
 & \text{OR-tree (Model I)} \ \longrightarrow\ \mathit{assign}_0 & \\[2pt]
 \swarrow & & \searrow\\[2pt]
 \text{Set-based (Model II)} & & \text{AND-tree (Model III)}\\
 \downarrow & & \downarrow\\
 \mathit{assign}_A & & \mathit{assign}_B\\[2pt]
 \text{Proposal A} & & \text{Proposal B}
\end{array}}
\label{eq:proposals}
\end{equation}

All three models use the problem definition of Section~\ref{sec:problem}: the components $X$, the domains $D(x)$, the overlap test \eqref{eq:overlap}, the checker $\mathrm{Consistent}$ \eqref{eq:consistent} and $\mathrm{Eval}$.

\textbf{Shared first stage:} Both second stages need a valid timetable to start from. The OR-tree (Section~4) finds one, $\mathit{assign}_0$, or reports that no valid timetable exists. It only looks for feasibility and ignores $\mathrm{Eval}$.

\textbf{How the stages connect:} Both second stages begin with $\mathit{assign}_0$ as their best timetable and only replace it with a strictly better valid one, so
\[
\mathrm{Eval}(\mathit{assign}_A) \le \mathrm{Eval}(\mathit{assign}_0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathrm{Eval}(\mathit{assign}_B) \le \mathrm{Eval}(\mathit{assign}_0).
\]
