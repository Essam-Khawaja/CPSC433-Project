\section{Solution 2: AND-Tree Optimization Following OR-Tree Feasibility}
\label{andopt:section}

The AND-tree stage minimizes soft penalties using branch-and-bound.
It receives a complete feasible timetable $a_{\mathrm{init}}$ from OR-tree
search and initializes the incumbent
$a_{\mathrm{best}}=a_{\mathrm{init}}$ and
$B=\operatorname{Eval}(a_{\mathrm{best}})$.
Only mandatory assignments $a_{\mathrm{fix}}$ are fixed at the search root;
the incumbent's other choices remain open to revision.
Every child case in a selected division must be explored or soundly
excluded before its parent is resolved. Child timetables are compared,
not merged.

\subsection{Search model}
The model is $A_{\land}=(S_{\land},T_{\land})$.

\paragraph{Problem descriptions and division.}
Let $X=L\cup T$ contain all lectures and tutorials/labs after shared
preprocessing, and let $D(x)\subseteq S$ be the typed slot domain of $x$.
Both stages use the same hard-constraint predicate $\operatorname{Constr}$
and full nonnegative objective $\operatorname{Eval}$.
For a partial assignment $a\supseteq a_{\mathrm{fix}}$, put
\[
 U(a)=X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a),\qquad
 D_a(x)=\{s\in D(x):
       \operatorname{Consistent}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s\})\}.
\]
Here $\operatorname{Consistent}$ rejects known hard violations without
rejecting any partial assignment having a feasible completion.
Define
\[
 \operatorname{Prob}_{\land}
 =\{\operatorname{Opt}(a,U(a)):a\text{ extends }a_{\mathrm{fix}}\}.
\]
The task $\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$ concerns all valid completions of $a$.
Write $V(a,U)$ for their minimum full penalty, or $+\infty$ if none exists.
For $x\in U$ with $D_a(x)=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}\ne\varnothing$, define
\begin{align*}
 p&=\operatorname{Opt}(a,U),&
 p_i&=\operatorname{Opt}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}),\\
 &&\operatorname{Div}(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k)&\text{ holds}.
\end{align*}
A selected division includes every legal slot case for $x$. These cases
partition the parent's feasible completions, so
\[
 V(a,U)=\min_i V(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}).
\]
Each child retains the full partial assignment, preserving shared capacities
and conflicts.

\paragraph{States, certificates, and transitions.}
States are whole AND-trees, $S_{\land}\subseteq Atree$, whose nodes contain
a problem $p$ and status $?\,$ or $\mathsf{yes}$.
A resolved node $v$ retains a certificate
$B\leq\ell_v\leq V(a,U)$; thus its case cannot improve the incumbent.
A complete valid timetable supplies its exact score after any incumbent
update, an infeasible case supplies $+\infty$, and a pruned case supplies
its lower bound. A parent retains the minimum of its children's certificates.
Consequently $\mathsf{yes}$ means resolved, not necessarily feasible.

The extension relation $\operatorname{Erw}_{\land}$ applies either rule
at a leaf within the surrounding tree:
\[
 (p,?)\longrightarrow(p,\mathsf{yes})
 \quad\text{if certified},\qquad
 (p,?)\longrightarrow(p,?,(p_1,?),\ldots,(p_k,?))
 \quad\text{if }\operatorname{Div}(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k).
\]
Writing $\operatorname{Erw}_{\land}^{*}$ for a finite sequence of extensions,
including zero steps, define
\[
 T_{\land}=\{(q,q')\in S_{\land}^{2}:
 \operatorname{Erw}_{\land}(q,q')
 \ \text{or}\ \operatorname{Erw}_{\land}^{*}(q',q)\}.
\]
The reversed relation permits contraction. Control contracts only resolved
subtrees after preserving their certificates and incumbent updates.

\subsection{Search process}
The process is $P_{\land}=(A_{\land},Env_{\land},K_{\land})$.

The environment stores the instance, incumbent and bound, node certificates,
traversal records, and fixed offering/slot orders for tie-breaking.

\paragraph{Lower bound and pruning.}
For a partial assignment $a$, count only penalties already fixed by its
assigned offerings:
\[
 \operatorname{LB}(a)=C_{\mathrm{pref}}(a)
       +p_{\mathrm{pair}}N_{\mathrm{pair}}(a)
       +p_{\mathrm{section}}N_{\mathrm{section}}(a).
\]
Here $C_{\mathrm{pref}}(a)$ is the total penalty of preference requests
already violated by assigned offerings.
The count $N_{\mathrm{pair}}(a)$ is the number of requested unordered
same-type pairs whose two offerings are already assigned to different
slots; reversed duplicates count once.
The count $N_{\mathrm{section}}(a)$ is the number of unordered pairs of
distinct lecture sections of the same department/course number whose two
offerings are already assigned to the same slot.
The constants $p_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{section}}$ are the
corresponding per-pair penalties. These constants and
$C_{\mathrm{pref}}$ include any applicable objective weights and use the
same counting conventions as $\operatorname{Eval}$; all contributions
are nonnegative.

Every completion $\widehat a\supseteq a$ preserves the assignments in $a$,
so none of these counted penalties can disappear within this branch.
Minimum-occupancy penalties are omitted because later assignments may
remove a shortfall, but remain in the full objective. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{LB}(a)\leq\operatorname{Eval}(\widehat a)
 \quad\text{for every feasible completion }\widehat a\supseteq a.
\]
Since $B=\operatorname{Eval}(a_{\mathrm{best}})$ is the score of an existing
complete feasible timetable, prune when $\operatorname{LB}(a)\geq B$:
no completion can improve the incumbent. Equality may also be pruned
because only one optimal timetable is required.

\paragraph{Control.}
The leaf selector $f_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ visits unresolved leaves depth-first,
ordering each division's children by increasing lower bound and then the
fixed slot order. The transition selector $f_{\mathrm{tran}}$ applies the
first applicable rule:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Reject a hard-inconsistent assignment.
 \item If complete, check all hard constraints, evaluate it, and replace
 the incumbent only if its score is smaller.
 \item Reject a case with an empty legal domain for any remaining offering.
 \item Prune if $\operatorname{LB}(a)\geq B$.
 \item Otherwise choose $x^*=\arg\min_{x\in U}|D_a(x)|$ (MRV), break ties
 by the fixed offering order, and expand into all its legal slot cases.
\end{enumerate}
Infeasible cases receive certificate $+\infty$; pruning retains
$\operatorname{LB}(a)$ rather than declaring infeasibility.
Combine certificates upward once all children are resolved.
Resolved cases are never explored again.

\subsection{Search instance, termination, and guarantees}

The instance is $\operatorname{Inst}_{\land}=(q_0,G_{\land})$, where
\[
 q_0=(\operatorname{Opt}(a_{\mathrm{fix}},
             X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a_{\mathrm{fix}})),?).
\]
The goal $G_{\land}(q)$ holds when the root is resolved: a leaf is resolved
when certified $\mathsf{yes}$, and an expanded task is resolved when all its
children are resolved and their certificates combined.
At termination,
\[
 B\leq\ell_{\mathrm{root}}
   \leq V(a_{\mathrm{fix}},U(a_{\mathrm{fix}}))\leq B,
\]
so the feasible incumbent is optimal. A zero-score incumbent is immediately
optimal by nonnegativity. A resource-limit interruption returns the incumbent
with optimality unproved. Finite domains and one new assignment per division
ensure termination without interruption; worst-case work remains exponential.

\subsection{Demonstration on a small search instance}
\label{andopt:demonstration}

\paragraph{Instance.}
Let $X=L=\{x,y,z\}$ be lectures of three different courses, $T=\varnothing$,
and $a_{\mathrm{fix}}=\varnothing$.
Each lecture can use $s_1=\text{MWF 09:00--10:00}$ or
$s_2=\text{MWF 10:00--11:00}$. Both slots have maximum 2 and minimum 0.
The only additional active hard restriction is
$\operatorname{noncompatible}(x,y)$, requiring $a(x)\ne a(y)$.
There are no pairing requests, same-course section pairs, or other active
restrictions. All three lectures prefer $s_1$, with penalties 4, 2, and 1,
respectively; all other soft terms vanish. Writing $\mathbf{1}[Q]$
for 1 when $Q$ holds and 0 otherwise,
\[
 \operatorname{Eval}(a)=4\mathbf{1}[a(x)=s_2]
       +2\mathbf{1}[a(y)=s_2]+\mathbf{1}[a(z)=s_2].
\]
Use tie orders $x<y<z$ and $s_1<s_2$.
Suppose OR-tree supplies the feasible incumbent
$a_{\mathrm{init}}=\{x\mapsto s_2,y\mapsto s_1,z\mapsto s_2\}$,
with $B=5$.

\paragraph{Expansion and traversal.}
Initially all domains have size 2, so MRV selects $x$. The root division is
\[
 \operatorname{Div}\bigl(
 \operatorname{Opt}(\varnothing,\{x,y,z\}),
 \operatorname{Opt}(\{x\mapsto s_1\},\{y,z\}),
 \operatorname{Opt}(\{x\mapsto s_2\},\{y,z\})\bigr).
\]
Its children have lower bounds 0 and 4, so $f_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ visits
$x\mapsto s_1$ first. Here $D_a(y)=\{s_2\}$ and
$D_a(z)=\{s_1,s_2\}$; MRV selects $y$, then divides on $z$.
The visit order after the root is:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c|ccc|c|p{6.0cm}}
Visit & $x$ & $y$ & $z$ & $\operatorname{LB}$ & Action \\ \hline
1 & $s_1$ & -- & -- & 0 & Expand on $y$; only $s_2$ is legal. \\
2 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & -- & 2 & Expand on $z$ into both legal slots. \\
3 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_1$ & 2 & Valid completion: update $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ and $B\gets2$. \\
4 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_2$ & 3 & Valid completion: retain incumbent of score 2. \\
5 & $s_2$ & -- & -- & 4 & Prune because $4\geq B=2$; retain certificate 4.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A dash denotes an unassigned offering. Visit 4 is evaluated because the
complete-assignment rule precedes pruning. Visit 5 generates no descendants:
the penalty 4 is already unavoidable.

\paragraph{Resolution.}
The two $z$ leaves return certificates 2 and 3. Their parent and the
single-child division on $y$ return 2. The root combines
$\ell_{\mathrm{root}}=\min(2,4)=2=B$, satisfies $G_{\land}$, and returns
$a_{\mathrm{best}}=\{x\mapsto s_1,y\mapsto s_2,z\mapsto s_1\}$ as optimal.
The four feasible timetables have scores 2, 3, 4, and 5; the bound accounts
for the final two without enumerating them during optimization.
