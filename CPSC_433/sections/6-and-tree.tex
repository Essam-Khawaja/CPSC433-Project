\section{Proposal B: AND-Tree Optimization Following OR-Tree Feasibility}
\label{andopt:section}

\subsection{Search model: $A_{\land}=(S_{\land},T_{\land})$}
The second stage receives a complete feasible timetable from OR-tree search and seeks a timetable with minimum soft-constraint penalty. We use the optimization interpretation of AND-tree search from Lecture 09: divide an optimization problem into cases, account for every case, and combine their results by comparison. Branch-and-bound avoids exploring a case when a sound lower bound proves that it cannot improve the best timetable already found.

Let $a_{\mathrm{init}}$ be the feasible timetable. The optimization stage initializes its incumbent and upper bound as
\begin{equation}
 a_{\mathrm{best}}=a_{\mathrm{init}},\qquad B=\Eval(a_{\mathrm{best}}).
 \label{andopt:incumbent}
\end{equation}
The incumbent is always complete and satisfies every hard constraint. Its assignments are not imposed on the optimization tree. Let $a_{\mathrm{fix}}$ contain the mandatory assignments, including special bookings when triggered. Define the problem descriptions and their feasible completions by
\begin{align}
 \Prob_{\land}&=\{\operatorname{Opt}(a,U): a\supseteq a_{\mathrm{fix}},\ U=X\setminus\domn(a)\},\label{andopt:prob}\\
 \mathcal F(a)&=\{\widehat a:X\to\mathit{Slots}:\widehat a\supseteq a,\ \widehat a(x)\in D(x),\ \Const(\widehat a)=\mathsf{true}\},\\
 V(a,U)&=\min_{\widehat a\in\mathcal F(a)}\Eval(\widehat a),\qquad \min\varnothing=+\infty.\label{andopt:value}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Division relation.}
For an unresolved problem $p=\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$, choose $x\in U$ and let $D_a(x)=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}$ be its currently legal slots. Each child fixes one slot:
\begin{equation}
 \Div(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k),\qquad
 p_i=\operatorname{Opt}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}).\label{andopt:div}
\end{equation}
These cases partition the feasible completions of the parent; all cases must be accounted for, unlike OR-tree feasibility, which stops after one success. Thus
\begin{equation}
 V(a,U)=\min_{1\le i\le k}V(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}).\label{andopt:combine}
\end{equation}
No independence between departments or courses is assumed: each child retains the full partial assignment.

\paragraph{Tree states and transitions.}
Following Lecture 09, $S_{\land}\subseteq\Atree$ and $T_{\land}\subseteq S_{\land}\times S_{\land}$. A leaf is $(p,sol)$; an internal node is $(p,sol,b_1,\ldots,b_k)$, with $sol\in\{\und,\yes\}$. We instantiate the extension relation by
\[
(p,\und)\longrightarrow (p,\yes)\quad\text{when certified},\qquad
(p,\und)\longrightarrow(p,\und,(p_1,\und),\ldots,(p_k,\und))\quad\text{when }\Div(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k).
\]
Extensions also act on leaves within larger trees; resolved subtrees may be contracted after retaining their result. These expansions and reverse contractions define $T_{\land}$, and the control never discards an unresolved case.

A resolved node $v$ with problem $\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$ retains a certificate $\ell_v$ such that
\begin{equation}
 B\le\ell_v\le V(a,U).\label{andopt:certificate}
\end{equation}
For a complete valid case, the certificate is its exact evaluation (updating $B$ first if necessary); for an infeasible case it is $+\infty$; for a pruned case it is $\LB(a)$; and when all children are resolved the parent retains $\min_i\ell_{v_i}$. A pruned case is \emph{dominated}, not necessarily infeasible. Since $B$ only decreases, retained certificates remain valid.

\subsection{Search process: $P_{\land}=(A_{\land},\Env_{\land},K_{\land})$}
The environment contains the scheduling instance, the checker $\mathrm{Consistent}$, $\Eval$, the incumbent $a_{\mathrm{best}}$, the bound $B$, node certificates and the depth-first traversal records.

\paragraph{Lower bound and pruning.}
Let $w(x,s)\ge0$ denote a stated preference penalty, $\mathcal P$ the requested unordered same-type offering pairs and $\mathcal R$ pairs of distinct lecture sections of the same course. For $A=\domn(a)$, the penalties already fixed by a partial assignment give
\begin{align}
 \LB(a)={}&\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{s\in\mathit{Slots}}w(x,s)\,\mathbf1[a(x)\ne s]\notag\\
 &+\mathit{pen}_{\mathit{notpaired}}\!\sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal P\\x,y\in A}}\mathbf1[a(x)\ne a(y)]\notag\\
 &+\mathit{pen}_{\mathit{section}}\!\sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal R\\x,y\in A}}\mathbf1[a(x)=a(y)].\label{andopt:lb}
\end{align}
Here $\mathbf1[Q]$ is 1 if $Q$ holds and 0 otherwise. Each counted penalty persists in every completion. Minimum-occupancy penalties are omitted because additional offerings may remove a shortfall; they remain part of the full $\Eval$. Thus for every $\widehat a\in\mathcal F(a)$,
\begin{equation}
 \LB(a)\le\Eval(\widehat a),\qquad
 \boxed{\LB(a)\ge B\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{prune the case}.}\label{andopt:prune}
\end{equation}
Equality may be pruned because we need only one optimum.

\paragraph{Search control.}
The control $K_{\land}$ selects the deepest unresolved leaf (depth-first search). It first resolves complete or inconsistent cases, then rejects a case with an empty legal domain or $\LB(a)\ge B$. Otherwise it selects the next component by minimum remaining values,
\begin{equation}
 x^*=\arg\min_{x\in U}|D_a(x)|,\qquad
 D_a(x)=\{s\in D(x):\mathrm{Consistent}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s\})\},\label{andopt:mrv}
\end{equation}
and divides into all legal slot cases. Children are visited by increasing lower bound, with fixed tie-breaks. After all children are resolved, their certificates are combined using~\eqref{andopt:combine}. Every division assigns one unassigned offering, so depth is at most $|X|$.

\paragraph{Search instance, goal and implementation.}
The instance begins at
\begin{equation}
 q_0=(\operatorname{Opt}(a_{\mathrm{fix}},X\setminus\domn(a_{\mathrm{fix}})),\und),\qquad
 G_{\land}(q)=\yes\iff\operatorname{Resolved}(q),\label{andopt:root}
\end{equation}
where a leaf is resolved when it holds a certificate and an internal node is resolved when all children are resolved. When the root is resolved, $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ is optimal; if interrupted by a resource limit, it remains feasible but is not proven optimal. If $B=0$, nonnegativity of $\Eval$ already proves optimality.

For implementation, a \textbf{dictionary} maps offerings to slots, \textbf{sets/lists} hold remaining offerings and legal slots, and a \textbf{depth-first stack} stores one active case per level: partial assignments (or a reversible assignment trail), remaining alternatives, lower bound and certificate. A second dictionary or array caches per-slot occupancies, while $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ and $B$ store the incumbent. Completed subtrees need not remain in memory after their certificates are retained.

\subsection{Worked AND-tree derivation}
\label{andopt:demonstration}
Let $X=\{x,y,z\}$ be lectures of different courses with $D(x)=D(y)=D(z)=\{s_1,s_2\}$, where $s_1$ and $s_2$ are MWF 9--10 and 10--11. Both slots have capacity two and minimum zero. The only incompatibility is between $x$ and $y$. All other penalties are zero except preferences $w(x,s_1)=4$, $w(y,s_1)=2$, $w(z,s_1)=1$. Thus
\[
 \Eval(a)=4\mathbf1[a(x)=s_2]+2\mathbf1[a(y)=s_2]+\mathbf1[a(z)=s_2].
\]
Suppose OR-tree search gives $a_{\mathrm{init}}=\{x\mapsto s_2,y\mapsto s_1,z\mapsto s_2\}$ with $B=5$. Starting from the empty assignment, MRV chooses $x$ (fixed tie-break). Division creates the $x\mapsto s_1$ and $x\mapsto s_2$ cases with lower bounds 0 and 4.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}c c c c c X@{}}
\toprule
Visit & $x$ & $y$ & $z$ & $\LB$ & Search-control action \\
\midrule
1 & $s_1$ & -- & -- & 0 & Select $y$; only $s_2$ is legal.\\
2 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & -- & 2 & Select $z$; both slots are legal.\\
3 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_1$ & 2 & Valid completion; update $B\gets2$.\\
4 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_2$ & 3 & Valid completion; incumbent stays 2.\\
5 & $s_2$ & -- & -- & 4 & Prune because $4\ge B=2$.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The resolved root retains $\ell_{\mathrm{root}}=\min(2,4)=2$. In general, complete partitions and sound certificates give
\[
 B\le\ell_{\mathrm{root}}\le V(a_{\mathrm{fix}},X\setminus\domn(a_{\mathrm{fix}}))\le B,
\]
so resolution proves global optimality. The example returns $\{x\mapsto s_1,y\mapsto s_2,z\mapsto s_1\}$ with score 2; the search prunes the remaining case without claiming it infeasible. Worst-case work remains exponential.
