\section{Solution 2: AND-Tree Optimization Following OR-Tree Feasibility}
\label{andopt:section}

\subsection{Purpose and connection to the feasibility stage}

The second stage receives a complete feasible timetable from OR-tree search
and seeks a timetable with minimum soft-constraint penalty. We use the
optimization interpretation of AND-tree search from Lecture 09: divide an
optimization problem into cases, account for every case, and combine their
results by comparison. Branch-and-bound avoids exploring a case when a sound
lower bound proves that it cannot improve the best timetable already found.

The children of a division represent different possible assignments of one
offering. Their timetables are compared, rather than combined into a single
assignment. All child cases must nevertheless be accounted for before the
parent's optimization task is resolved. This differs from the preceding
OR-tree feasibility stage, which may stop after finding one valid completion.

Let $a_{\mathrm{init}}$ be the timetable returned by the feasibility stage.
The optimization stage initializes its incumbent and upper bound as
\begin{equation}
 a_{\mathrm{best}}=a_{\mathrm{init}},
 \qquad B=\operatorname{Eval}(a_{\mathrm{best}}).
 \label{andopt:incumbent}
\end{equation}
The incumbent is always complete and satisfies every hard constraint. Its
assignments are not imposed on the optimization tree. Only assignments fixed
by the input or required by the problem specification are fixed at the root.
If the feasibility stage proves that no valid timetable exists, this stage
is not invoked. If that stage stops without finding a timetable or proving
infeasibility, its result is inconclusive and does not establish that the
input is infeasible.

\subsection{Scheduling notation and shared functions}

Let $L$ be the set of lecture sections, $T$ the set of tutorials and labs,
and $X=L\cup T$. Let $S$ be the set of typed abstract weekly slots and
$D(x)\subseteq S$ the domain of offering $x$. A slot records its offering
type, recurring days, and time interval. Actual time overlap is used for
hard conflict checks, including when lecture and tutorial durations differ.

An assignment $a$ is a partial function from $X$ to $S$, with
$a(x)\in D(x)$ for each $x\in\operatorname{dom}(a)$. Define
\begin{equation}
 U(a)=X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a).
\end{equation}
A complete assignment $\widehat a$ extends $a$, written $\widehat a\supseteq a$,
when it agrees with every assignment already recorded in $a$.

We use $\operatorname{Constr}(\widehat a)$ for the conjunction of all hard
constraints in the official problem statement. We use
$\operatorname{Consistent}(a)$ for a sound partial-assignment check:
it rejects known violations without rejecting a partial assignment that has
a feasible completion. Passing this check does not prove that a feasible
completion exists. For complete assignments, the check agrees with
$\operatorname{Constr}$. Both stages use the same domains, constraint
interpretations, and full evaluation function.

Let $a_{\mathrm{fix}}$ contain all mandatory assignments. Preprocessing
creates the CPSC 851 tutorial when a CPSC 351 lecture is present and the
CPSC 913 tutorial when a CPSC 413 lecture is present. Each required special
tutorial is created once, is fixed to Tuesday/Thursday 18:00--19:00, and
counts toward the ordinary tutorial capacity. The prescribed conflicts with
its associated course are included in $\operatorname{Constr}$. These
generated offerings are included in $T$ before either search begins.
All partial assignments considered below extend $a_{\mathrm{fix}}$.

For $x\in U(a)$, define its currently legal slots by
\begin{equation}
 D_a(x)=\{s\in D(x):
 \operatorname{Consistent}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s\})\}.
 \label{andopt:legal}
\end{equation}
The full objective $\operatorname{Eval}(\widehat a)$ is the sum of the
specified minimum-lecture-usage, minimum-tutorial-usage, preference, pairing,
and lecture-section penalties. Every term is nonnegative. A low objective
value never compensates for a hard-constraint violation.

\subsection{Search model}
The model is $A_{\land}=(S_{\land},T_{\land})$.

\paragraph{Problem descriptions.}
Define
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Prob}_{\land}
 =\{\operatorname{Opt}(a,U):
       a\text{ is a partial assignment extending }a_{\mathrm{fix}},
       U=U(a)\}.
 \label{andopt:prob}
\end{equation}
The description $\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$ asks us to account for all valid
completions of $a$ when searching for a minimum-penalty timetable. Its
underlying optimization value is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal F(a)&=\{\widehat a:X\to S:
       \widehat a\supseteq a,
       \operatorname{Constr}(\widehat a)=\mathsf{true}\},\\
 V(a,U)&=\min_{\widehat a\in\mathcal F(a)}
                  \operatorname{Eval}(\widehat a),
 \qquad \min\varnothing=+\infty.
 \label{andopt:value}
\end{align}
The value $+\infty$ denotes absence of a feasible completion. It is not an
additional soft penalty. The bounded search need not compute the exact value
of every subproblem: a certified lower bound can suffice to exclude a case
from improving the global incumbent.

\paragraph{Division relation.}
For an unresolved problem $p=\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$, choose $x\in U$.
If $D_a(x)=\{s_1,\ldots,s_k\}$ with $k\geq1$, define
\begin{align}
 p_i&=\operatorname{Opt}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}),
          &&1\leq i\leq k,\\
 \operatorname{Div}(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k)&\quad\text{holds}.
 \label{andopt:div}
\end{align}
Each choice of the offering $x$ gives a possible division. Once selected,
that division contains every currently legal slot case for $x$. It does not
select just one slot. These cases partition the feasible completions of the
parent, so
\begin{equation}
 V(a,U)=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}
 V(a\cup\{x\mapsto s_i\},U\setminus\{x\}).
 \label{andopt:combine}
\end{equation}
The full partial assignment is retained in every child; shared capacities
and conflicts between offerings are therefore preserved. No independence
between departments or courses is assumed. If any unassigned offering has
an empty legal domain, then $\mathcal F(a)=\varnothing$ and the case can be
resolved without a division.

\paragraph{Tree states and resolution certificates.}
As in the lecture, states are whole AND-trees, $S_{\land}\subseteq Atree$.
A leaf is $(p,\mathit{sol})$ and an internal node is
$(p,\mathit{sol},b_1,\ldots,b_k)$, where
$p\in\operatorname{Prob}_{\land}$ and
$\mathit{sol}\in\{?,\mathsf{yes}\}$.
The environment stores the incumbent and the certificates associated with
tree nodes.

For this optimization instantiation, $\mathsf{yes}$ means that the node's
case is resolved for the global minimization task. It does not necessarily
mean that the case contains a feasible timetable or that its exact optimum
was computed. A resolved node $v$ with problem $\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$
has a retained certificate $\ell_v$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 B\leq\ell_v\leq V(a,U).
 \label{andopt:certificate}
\end{equation}
Thus no completion in that case improves the current incumbent. Certificates
are obtained in the following ways:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For a complete valid assignment, evaluate it and update the incumbent
 if its penalty is smaller. Then take $\ell_v=\operatorname{Eval}(a)$.
 \item For a case proved infeasible, take $\ell_v=+\infty$.
 \item For a case pruned by a sound lower bound, take $\ell_v=\operatorname{LB}(a)$.
 \item If every child of a division is resolved, take
 $\ell_v=\min_i\ell_{v_i}$. Equation~\eqref{andopt:combine} makes this a
 sound certificate for the parent.
\end{enumerate}
The incumbent value only decreases, so certificates remain valid after a
later improvement. A pruned case is recorded as dominated, not incorrectly
reported as infeasible or assigned an exact value of $+\infty$.

\paragraph{Expansion and contraction.}
We instantiate the lecture's extension operations as follows:
\begin{align}
 (p,?)&\longrightarrow(p,\mathsf{yes})
       &&\text{when a certificate is available},\\
 (p,?)&\longrightarrow(p,?,(p_1,?),\ldots,(p_k,?))
       &&\text{when }\operatorname{Div}(p,p_1,\ldots,p_k).
 \label{andopt:extension}
\end{align}
These local operations, and their application within an unchanged surrounding
tree, define $\operatorname{Erw}_{\land}$. A solved leaf is not expanded again.
For this section, $\operatorname{Erw}_{\land}^{*}$ denotes a finite sequence
of such extensions, including the zero-step sequence. Reversing an extension
sequence gives a structural contraction:
\begin{equation}
 T_{\land}=\{(q,q')\in S_{\land}\times S_{\land}:
   \operatorname{Erw}_{\land}(q,q')
   \ \text{or}\ 
   \operatorname{Erw}_{\land}^{*}(q',q)\}.
 \label{andopt:transitions}
\end{equation}
The argument order in the second relation expresses contraction. The control
permits contraction of a resolved subtree only after retaining its aggregate
certificate and any incumbent update. It may then mark the resulting leaf
$\mathsf{yes}$. It never erases an unresolved case or repeats a completed
division. Ordinary depth-first return proceeds to the next unresolved
sibling. Infeasible children resolve to $+\infty$; they do not force the
entire parent optimization problem to fail.

\subsection{Search process}
The process is $P_{\land}=(A_{\land},Env_{\land},K_{\land})$.

\paragraph{Environment.}
The environment contains the fixed scheduling instance, $D$, the shared
constraint checker and objective, $a_{\mathrm{best}}$, $B$, node certificates,
and traversal records. Fixed total orders on offerings and slots give
deterministic tie-breaking. The environment also records whether processing
finished normally or was interrupted by a specified resource limit.

\paragraph{A sound lower bound.}
Let $w(x,s)\geq0$ be the input preference value attached to offering $x$
and preferred slot $s$, or zero if no such preference is supplied. Let
$\mathcal P$ be the set of requested unordered offering pairs, with reversed
duplicates counted once. By the instructor's clarification, each pair
contains two lectures or two tutorials/labs. A pairing is satisfied exactly
when their assigned slots are equal. Let $\mathcal R$ contain every unordered
pair of distinct lecture sections with the same department and course number.

Write $\mathbf{1}[Q]$ for 1 when proposition $Q$ is true and 0 otherwise,
and put $A=\operatorname{dom}(a)$. Define
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{LB}(a)
 ={}&\sum_{x\in A}\sum_{s\in S} w(x,s)\,
                       \mathbf{1}[a(x)\ne s]\\
 &+\operatorname{pen}_{\mathrm{notpaired}}
      \sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal P\\x,y\in A}}
                       \mathbf{1}[a(x)\ne a(y)]\\
 &+\operatorname{pen}_{\mathrm{section}}
      \sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal R\\x,y\in A}}
                       \mathbf{1}[a(x)=a(y)].
 \label{andopt:lb}
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{pen}_{\mathrm{section}}$ denotes the specified penalty
for two sections of the same course sharing a slot. All counted penalties
are fixed by assignments already made and persist in every extension.
Minimum-occupancy penalties are omitted because additional offerings may
remove the current shortfall. They are still included when computing the
full $\operatorname{Eval}$ of a complete timetable. Since the remaining
objective contributions are nonnegative,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{LB}(a)\leq\operatorname{Eval}(\widehat a)
 \quad\text{for every }\widehat a\in\mathcal F(a).
 \label{andopt:lb-sound}
\end{equation}
Consequently the pruning condition is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{LB}(a)\geq B.
 \label{andopt:prune}
\end{equation}
Equality is sufficient because the task is to return one minimum-penalty
timetable, rather than enumerate all timetables tied for the minimum.

\paragraph{Leaf selection: $f_{\mathrm{leaf}}$.}
Select the next unresolved leaf in a depth-first traversal. When a division
is first created, order its children by increasing
$\operatorname{LB}(a\cup\{x\mapsto s\})$, breaking ties by the fixed slot
order. Fully process the first child before continuing to its next sibling.
This seeks a useful complete incumbent early while avoiding a global
best-first frontier. Resolved leaves are excluded from selection.

\paragraph{Transition selection: $f_{\mathrm{tran}}$.}
For the selected leaf $\operatorname{Opt}(a,U)$, apply the first applicable
rule in the following order:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $\operatorname{Consistent}(a)$ is false, resolve the case as
 infeasible.
 \item If $U=\varnothing$, check $\operatorname{Constr}(a)$. Evaluate and
 possibly replace the incumbent if valid; otherwise resolve as infeasible.
 \item If some $x\in U$ has $D_a(x)=\varnothing$, resolve as infeasible.
 \item If $\operatorname{LB}(a)\geq B$, retain the lower-bound certificate
 and resolve the case without expansion.
 \item Otherwise select the division offering by minimum remaining values:
 \begin{equation}
 x^*=\arg\min_{x\in U}|D_a(x)|,
 \label{andopt:mrv}
 \end{equation}
 breaking ties by the fixed offering order. Expand into all legal slot
 cases for $x^*$ using~\eqref{andopt:div}.
\end{enumerate}
After a case is resolved, combine certificates upward whenever all children
of a node have been resolved. No alternative division by another offering
is required: the chosen division already covers every feasible completion.
Together these rules define $K_{\land}$.

\subsection{Search instance and termination}

The search instance is $\operatorname{Inst}_{\land}=(q_0,G_{\land})$, where
\begin{equation}
 q_0=(\operatorname{Opt}(a_{\mathrm{fix}},
                 X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a_{\mathrm{fix}})),?).
 \label{andopt:root}
\end{equation}
Initialize the environment by~\eqref{andopt:incumbent}. Define tree resolution
recursively by
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Resolved}((p,\mathsf{yes}))&=\mathsf{true},\\
 \operatorname{Resolved}((p,?))&=\mathsf{false},\\
 \operatorname{Resolved}((p,?,b_1,\ldots,b_k))
     &=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Resolved}(b_i).
\end{align}
Every $\mathsf{yes}$ marking must be supported by a retained certificate.
For reachable states, the goal predicate is
\begin{equation}
 G_{\land}(q)=\operatorname{Resolved}(q).
\end{equation}
The comparison operation in~\eqref{andopt:combine} provides the required
compatibility of child results: they concern an exhaustive partition of the
parent's completion set. Their distinct timetable assignments need not
coexist in a single schedule.

When the root is resolved, return $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ as optimal. A zero
incumbent permits immediate termination: all objective terms are
nonnegative, so the root has the certificate $\ell_{\mathrm{root}}=0$.
If a resource limit interrupts the search before resolution, return the
incumbent as feasible, with optimality not established. Such interruption
is an external stopping condition, not satisfaction of $G_{\land}$.

\subsection{Operational procedure}

The following recursive procedure implements the selected divisions and
depth-first control. The incumbent is shared across recursive calls.
The return value is a certificate, not necessarily the exact optimum of
that subproblem. An interruption propagates immediately to the driver;
unfinished parents receive no certificate.

\begin{andoptalgorithm}{AND-Tree Branch-and-Bound: Recursive Search}
\textbf{Require:} Partial assignment $a$, remaining offerings $U$;
shared incumbent $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ and bound $B$.\\
\textbf{Ensure:} Return a certificate, or propagate interruption.
\andoptline{0}{\textbf{function} $\operatorname{OptimizeCase}(a,U)$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} resource limit reached \textbf{then} interrupt}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} $\neg\operatorname{Consistent}(a)$ \textbf{then return} $\infty$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} $U=\varnothing$ \textbf{then}}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{if} $\neg\operatorname{Constr}(a)$ \textbf{then return} $\infty$}
\andoptline{2}{$v\gets\operatorname{Eval}(a)$}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{if} $v<B$ \textbf{then}}
\andoptline{3}{$a_{\mathrm{best}}\gets\operatorname{copy}(a)$}
\andoptline{3}{$B\gets v$}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{end if}}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{return} $v$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{end if}}
\andoptline{1}{Compute $D_a(x)$ for every $x\in U$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} some $D_a(x)=\varnothing$ \textbf{then return} $\infty$}
\andoptline{1}{$b\gets\operatorname{LB}(a)$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} $b\ge B$ \textbf{then return} $b$}
\andoptline{1}{$x\gets$ MRV offering, with fixed tie-breaking}
\andoptline{1}{$L\gets D_a(x)$, ordered by child lower bound, then slot order}
\andoptline{1}{$c\gets\infty$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{for each} $s\in L$ \textbf{do}}
\andoptline{2}{$a'\gets a\cup\{x\mapsto s\}$}
\andoptline{2}{$c'\gets\operatorname{OptimizeCase}(a',U\setminus\{x\})$}
\andoptline{2}{$c\gets\min(c,c')$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{end for}}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{return} $c$}
\andoptline{0}{\textbf{end function}}
\end{andoptalgorithm}

\begin{andoptalgorithm}{AND-Tree Optimization: Driver}
\textbf{Require:} Instance $I$ and complete timetable $a_{\mathrm{init}}$
with $\operatorname{Constr}(a_{\mathrm{init}})=\mathsf{true}$.\\
\textbf{Ensure:} Return the best feasible timetable found and its optimality status.
\andoptline{0}{\textbf{function} $\operatorname{Driver}(I,a_{\mathrm{init}})$}
\andoptline{1}{$a_{\mathrm{best}}\gets\operatorname{copy}(a_{\mathrm{init}})$}
\andoptline{1}{$B\gets\operatorname{Eval}(a_{\mathrm{best}})$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{if} $B=0$ \textbf{then return} $(a_{\mathrm{best}},\mathsf{OPTIMAL})$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{try}}
\andoptline{2}{$\operatorname{OptimizeCase}(a_{\mathrm{fix}},X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a_{\mathrm{fix}}))$}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{return} $(a_{\mathrm{best}},\mathsf{OPTIMAL})$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{on interruption}}
\andoptline{2}{\textbf{return} $(a_{\mathrm{best}},\mathsf{FEASIBLE\_OPTIMALITY\_NOT\_ESTABLISHED})$}
\andoptline{1}{\textbf{end try}}
\andoptline{0}{\textbf{end function}}
\end{andoptalgorithm}

\subsection{Correctness and limitations}

\paragraph{Feasibility and preservation of the incumbent.}
The initial incumbent is feasible by the first stage's contract. It is
replaced only by a complete assignment passing $\operatorname{Constr}$.
Therefore every returned timetable is feasible and its penalty is no larger
than that of $a_{\mathrm{init}}$.

\paragraph{Coverage, pruning, and optimality.}
For any selected offering, every feasible completion belongs to exactly one
child slot case. Sound partial consistency checking never removes a feasible
completion. Equation~\eqref{andopt:lb-sound} justifies every bound-based
pruning decision. Terminal and combined certificates satisfy
\eqref{andopt:certificate}; decreases in $B$ preserve previously obtained
certificates. When the root is resolved,
\begin{equation}
 B\leq\ell_{\mathrm{root}}
   \leq V(a_{\mathrm{fix}},U(a_{\mathrm{fix}}))\leq B.
\end{equation}
The final inequality holds because the incumbent is itself a feasible
completion of $a_{\mathrm{fix}}$. Hence $B$ equals the global minimum.

\paragraph{Termination and cost.}
Every division assigns one previously unassigned offering, so branch depth
is at most $|X\setminus\operatorname{dom}(a_{\mathrm{fix}})|$. Domains are
finite, and the control never revisits a resolved case. Without interruption,
the search therefore terminates. Branch-and-bound can reduce exploration,
but worst-case work remains exponential in the number of offerings. MRV and
child ordering change exploration order without sacrificing coverage. The
initial feasible timetable supplies both a usable result on interruption and
an upper bound from the beginning; a better initial bound may permit more
pruning. A depth-first implementation can release resolved subtree detail
after preserving its certificate, rather than retain the entire explored
tree. No claim of consistently fast execution or optimality under a time
limit is made.

\subsection{Demonstration on a small search instance}
\label{andopt:demonstration}

This instance demonstrates the AND-tree stage of Solution~2, using a
feasible timetable supplied by the preceding OR-tree stage. It illustrates
division, leaf and transition selection, incumbent improvement, pruning,
and resolution of the root.

\paragraph{Instance and initial incumbent.}
Let $X=L=\{x,y,z\}$ and $T=\varnothing$, where $x,y,z$ are lecture
sections of three different courses. There are two lecture slots,
$s_1=\text{MWF 09:00--10:00}$ and
$s_2=\text{MWF 10:00--11:00}$, with
$D(x)=D(y)=D(z)=\{s_1,s_2\}$.
Each slot has lecture maximum 2 and lecture minimum 0.
The only additional active hard restriction is
$\operatorname{noncompatible}(x,y)$, so $a(x)\ne a(y)$.
There are no mandatory assignments, unwanted slots, active-learning
requirements, pairing requests, same-course section pairs, or special
course bookings; all other hard restrictions are inactive for this
illustrative instance. Thus $a_{\mathrm{fix}}=\varnothing$.

The preference data are
\[
 w(x,s_1)=4,\qquad w(y,s_1)=2,\qquad w(z,s_1)=1,
\]
with all other preference values zero. Because every other soft term
vanishes here, a complete feasible timetable has score
\[
 \operatorname{Eval}(a)
 =4\mathbf{1}[a(x)=s_2]
  +2\mathbf{1}[a(y)=s_2]
  +\mathbf{1}[a(z)=s_2].
\]
The lower bound counts exactly those preference penalties already fixed
by a partial assignment. Use offering order $x<y<z$ and slot order
$s_1<s_2$ for ties.

Suppose the OR-tree stage returns
\[
 a_{\mathrm{init}}=\{x\mapsto s_2,y\mapsto s_1,z\mapsto s_2\},
 \qquad B=\operatorname{Eval}(a_{\mathrm{init}})=5.
\]
This timetable satisfies the hard restrictions: $x$ and $y$ use different
slots, and the largest occupancy is 2. Its choices supply an upper bound;
they do not fix the optimization root.

\paragraph{First division and depth-first selection.}
The initial state is
$q_0=(\operatorname{Opt}(\varnothing,\{x,y,z\}),?)$.
Its lower bound is 0 and all three legal domains have size 2.
Consequently $f_{\mathrm{tran}}$ selects $x$ by MRV with the fixed tie-break,
and $\operatorname{Erw}_{\land}$ expands the root using
\[
 \operatorname{Div}\bigl(
 \operatorname{Opt}(\varnothing,\{x,y,z\}),
 \operatorname{Opt}(\{x\mapsto s_1\},\{y,z\}),
 \operatorname{Opt}(\{x\mapsto s_2\},\{y,z\})
 \bigr).
\]
These two child cases have lower bounds 0 and 4, respectively.
They both belong to the selected division. The control must account for
both, although it need not enumerate every completion of a pruned child.
The depth-first $f_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ selects the $x\mapsto s_1$ child first.

Here $D_a(y)=\{s_2\}$ because $x$ and $y$ are noncompatible,
whereas $D_a(z)=\{s_1,s_2\}$. MRV therefore selects $y$ and creates the
single legal child $y\mapsto s_2$.
For $a=\{x\mapsto s_1,y\mapsto s_2\}$, the lower bound is 2,
which is below the current $B=5$.
Both slots remain legal for $z$, giving two complete child cases with
lower bounds 2 and 3.

\newpage
\paragraph{Incumbent update and pruning.}
The following table records the recursive visit order after the root.
A dash indicates that an offering is still unassigned.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c|ccc|c|p{6.0cm}}
Visit & $x$ & $y$ & $z$ & $\operatorname{LB}$ & Action \\ \hline
1 & $s_1$ & -- & -- & 0 & Expand on $y$; only $s_2$ is legal. \\
2 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & -- & 2 & Expand on $z$ into both legal slots. \\
3 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_1$ & 2 & Complete and valid: replace the incumbent; $B\gets2$. \\
4 & $s_1$ & $s_2$ & $s_2$ & 3 & Complete and valid: score 3; keep the incumbent. \\
5 & $s_2$ & -- & -- & 4 & Prune: $4\ge B=2$; retain certificate 4.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At visit 3 the incumbent becomes
$a_{\mathrm{best}}=\{x\mapsto s_1,y\mapsto s_2,z\mapsto s_1\}$.
Visit 4 is evaluated because the controller checks complete assignments
before its lower-bound pruning rule. Its certificate is its exact score 3.
After resolving the first root child, depth-first traversal reaches the
$x\mapsto s_2$ child. Its preference penalty of 4 is already unavoidable,
so no completion below it can improve the incumbent of score 2.
The controller marks that case resolved with certificate 4 and generates
no descendants there. Pruning does not declare it infeasible.

\paragraph{Combining results and stopping.}
The two $z$ leaves return certificates 2 and 3.
Their parent retains $\min(2,3)=2$; the single-child division on $y$
also returns 2. The root then combines its two child certificates:
\[
 \ell_{\mathrm{root}}=\min(2,4)=2=B.
\]
Every selected child case is now resolved, so the root can be marked
$\mathsf{yes}$ and $G_{\land}$ holds. Resolved subtree detail may be
contracted after preserving these certificates and the incumbent.
The driver returns $a_{\mathrm{best}}$ with status $\mathsf{OPTIMAL}$.

As an independent check, the four feasible assignments have scores
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 (a(x),a(y),a(z))&
 (s_1,s_2,s_1)&(s_1,s_2,s_2)&(s_2,s_1,s_1)&(s_2,s_1,s_2)\\ \hline
 \operatorname{Eval}(a)&2&3&4&5
 \end{array}
\]
and hence the minimum is indeed 2. The search did not need to enumerate
the final two assignments during optimization: the retained bound
accounted for their entire root child case.
